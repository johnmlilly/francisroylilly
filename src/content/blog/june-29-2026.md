Hi everyone,

We wanted to provide an update on Francis on a few fronts.

As we mentioned in our last update, he attended Richmond Hope Therapy in late April every day for three weeks. A last-minute opening came available and we quickly rearranged our schedules and family routines to make it work. It was totally worth it! Francis made a lot of great improvements with his transitional movements and overall development. What makes Richmond Hope so special is that they specialize in exactly this type of intensive care. His PT and OT were so patient, encouraging, and committed to meeting Francis exactly where he is but also challenging him in ways they knew he could handle.

We are still praying and working toward crawling and standing, as he is making incremental progress in those areas. We were generously gifted a stander from some dear friends, which has been a huge help in ensuring his hips develop properly as he builds up the strength to stand on his own. He uses it for one to two hours a day when we're able to manage it, and we're hopeful it will make a real difference in his overall development.

Shortly after completing his intensive therapy, Francis hit another major milestone — the removal of his G-tube button! Kara advocated for this at his appointment after his therapists noticed he seemed uncomfortable on his tummy. Sure enough, once it was out, he became much more at ease on his stomach as he works on the skills to crawl. He still prefers to roll onto his back, but we're working on regular tummy time and encouraging him into sitting and crawling positions. The one ongoing concern is that the opening in his stomach has been slow to close and has continued to leak over the past several weeks. If it doesn't close on its own, he will need surgery to prevent infection and other complications. We're hoping to avoid that.

While all of these milestones are always huge for Francis, one update that hits home is that he is almost completely weaned off of his seizure medication. This is noteworthy since the very reason we had planned to say goodbye to him during Holy Week of last year was because of the inability to wean him off of seizure medications for fear of him having detrimental seizures. We are so grateful for the peace we have had weaning the medication over the last two months. It was very nerve-racking at first since it brought back some anxious memories, but with many prayers God continues to give us the grace to trust in Him.

A challenge we’ve been facing lately is with our insurance. Francis has a hard limit of visits per calendar year through our primary insurance. While he had qualified for Medicaid following his NICU stay as a secondary insurance, that coverage ended after one year. We’re exploring a Virginia Medicaid waiver as an option to see if he can be readmitted to the program but that is taking some time. As incredible as the Richmond Hope intensive was, it used a significant number of those visits. We have spent countless hours on the phone with our insurance, his doctors, and various providers trying to find a path forward. We’ve been denied, bounced around, or simply told there is nothing more that can be done. It has been exhausting and discouraging to say the least. We know God will be provide as he always does but we could use prayers for perseverance and guidance as we navigate this situation!

Thank you for continuing to follow our family’s journey through all of this. We are so grateful for your continued support and prayers.

Specific Prayer Requests:

- That Francis's G-tube site will heal completely on its own and that surgery will not be needed
- For peace and trust as we navigate insurance, therapy costs, and medical care
- That Francis will begin crawling and walking on his own

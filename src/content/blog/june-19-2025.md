First off, a very late happy Father’s Day to the many good fathers out there but especially to my beloved, John, who has learned quickly what it means to be a humble and sacrificial father. Seeing you hold our beloved Francis on Father’s Day in our own home was a blessing not everyone would understand.

Francis turned 2 months old on June 1st. I remember feeling a bit guilty that day since I did not take milestone photos similar to the girls. However, reflecting on what his 2 month meant to us made me realize that every single day is a milestone for this little one!

The past few weeks have been so surreal. Sometimes it feels like our stay in the NICU was all a dream now that we have our precious Francis at home. I shared with John the other day how easy it can be to forget what God has done for us since he is doing so well but we don’t want to forget. We hope and pray to continue witnessing that Francis’ recovery has been done by God’s hand and through others as His instruments.

While we get distracted by the chaos of taking care of three young children, we are reminded of God’s goodness when we see how family, friends and people in this community respond to seeing him in person. We are so proud to be his parents and see the impact he has made on so many lives.

One of the greatest highlights of having him home was bringing him to the cemetery for the first time to visit my dad, his Grandpa Roy Hollis or Lolo, on Father’s day. I am certain my dad is interceding for his grandson despite his desire to hold him.

Francis has been doing well overall. We have adjusted well to feeding him through his g-tube and he continues to improve in bottle and breast feeding with his limited intakes. Overall though, he is a pretty normal newborn - he eats, he poops, and he sleeps! We have even gotten a few smiles out of him. :)

We have already had several follow-up appointments and will have many more ahead of us in addition to therapies. He had his first Post-NICU speech evaluation last week to see how he is doing with bottle-feeding. His therapist said she did not see any visible signs of aspiration and he was coordinating well. She ended up allowing him to increase his bottle-feeds from 10mL to 15mL and we have planned for his next swallow study in early September!

Yesterday, he went to see his rehabilitation doctor. She has begun to assess him for cerebral palsy. His initial assessment indicated “high suspicion” of CP but he will do a repeat assessment a month from now.

We continue to pray for Francis’ full healing and recovery of all brain functions. However, we also understand that God may be calling us to live a different life of witness since Francis is at very high risk for impairments. We know that God will give us the grace for every moment and every circumstance that he calls us but we could use prayers for peace and trust in that!

Prayer Requests:

- For Francis to show improvements in gross motor skills and respond well as he begins physical therapies
- For Francis to continue progressing with bottle and breast feeding and that he is no longer silently aspirating to prevent future infections
- For Francis' brain to grow larger and show through his head shape
- For babies Cecilia and the newborn son of another friend
- For the prayer requests of baby Estevan’s parents: for guidance and strength, for Estevan to better manage his secretions and airway, for him to not have anymore spells, and for his swallowing to improve

Yesterday Francis went to pediatric radiology for his swallow study to determine whether or not he is silently aspirating while bottle and breast feeding. It was determined that he is having aspirations while feeding. This means that in order to bring him home sooner and to prevent the risk of pneumonia, he will need to undergo surgery for a gastrointestinal feeding tube. While this is not the answer to prayer that we were hoping for, we are so grateful for the clearer timeline of finally bringing him home. After many conversations with the nurses and friends who have gone through something similar, we realize that the G-tube can be a blessing in the long run. God has made the choice clear for us and now we have a further opportunity to trust that Francis' future continues to be in His hands.

In fact, that prayer request to get him home as soon as possible seems to be in the works! As soon as we conversed with his doctor to get his surgery scheduled, they shared that there is an opening for tomorrow (Wednesday) morning. This means that we may be bringing Francis home as early as next week!

Since things are shifting rather quickly, we've had some beautiful conversations with Francis' nurses. Thinking about going home is bittersweet since we've been incredibly blessed to have them as a major part of Francis' life. It is also clear that what God has done for our family has made an impact on them and others working at the hospital. This was a prayer that we have had since the beginning, that his life would be a witness to all those around him and caring for him.

The past couple of days have reminded us of what we focused on in the early days of Francis' life - that God always answers our prayers in His time and with love and mercy.

Please keep Francis in your prayers tonight and tomorrow as he prepares for and undergoes surgery!

Prayers requests:

- For a peaceful night as Francis preps for surgery (no feedings, IV fluids)
- For a successful surgery and recovery with no complications
- For a quick and smooth recovery from sedation and intubation
